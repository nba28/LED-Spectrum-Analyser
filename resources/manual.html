LED Spectrum Analyser

Visualizer for Apple Music and iTunes · version 3.1 · originally by Graham Cox (1999–2014)

A hi-fi style spectrum analyser: LED bargraphs showing level and peaks in 10 to 31 frequency bands per channel, optional VU bargraphs, or a pair of analogue VU meters. Version 3.1 recreates the look and behaviour of Graham Cox's LED Spectrum Analyser 3.0.7 as a 64-bit plug-in that runs natively on Apple Silicon and on Intel.

Installing

Quit Music (or iTunes). Copy LED Spectrum Analyser.bundle into ~/Library/iTunes/iTunes Plug-ins/ (the folder name is the same for Music; create it if it doesn't exist). Remove any older copy of LED Spectrum Analyser from that folder first. Then open Music, choose Window › Visualizer (or View › Visualizer), and pick LED Spectrum Analyser from the Visualizer menu.

The bundle is universal. Apple Music on Apple Silicon uses the native arm64 code; iTunes 10.7 (for example as installed by Retroactive) runs it as x86_64 under Rosetta; Intel Macs run it natively.

If macOS says the bundle is from an unidentified developer or damaged after downloading, remove the quarantine flag: xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine ~/Library/iTunes/iTunes\ Plug-ins/LED\ Spectrum\ Analyser.bundle

Options

Open the options with Visualizer › Options… where the host has it, by right-clicking the visualizer and choosing Visualizer Options…, or by typing d. Changes apply immediately and are remembered. With the options window open, 1 2 3 select its tabs.

Layout

LayoutSide By Side (bars rise from the bottom, frequency across), Back To Back (bars grow outwards from the centre, frequency up the middle) or Analogue VU Meters.
Bands10, 18, 24 or 31 bands per channel, spaced logarithmically from 20 Hz to 20 kHz (31 bands is about a third of an octave each).
VU BargraphsOverall level of each channel, under the spectrum.
Scale LabelsFrequency and level scales.
Progress BarElapsed and total time with a glowing progress bar.
Track InformationChoose any of Title, Artist, Album, Year and Station Ident (the station name for streamed audio). The text fades after 20 seconds unless Stay Visible is on. Above Display swaps it with the progress bar. Size To Fit shrinks long lines to fit; otherwise they are truncated.
Cover ArtShow displays the artwork for six seconds, after which it fades out or, with Use as Background Image, becomes a dim background. Use Artwork Colours takes the background and bar colours from the artwork.

Appearance

ColoursPeak, bar and blend colours for the spectrum and the VU bargraphs, and the background. Click a swatch to use the standard colour panel; colours may be partly transparent.
BlendBars shade from the bar colour to the blend colour. Blend to Current Value puts the blend colour at the top lit segment instead of at full scale.
RandomiseNew random colours whenever the track changes.
Animated ColoursAll colours slowly rotate through the spectrum.
DisplayPeak Indicators, Unlit Segments (the dark LEDs above the bars), Reflections and Perspective (each channel turned slightly away).
PresetsSave Preset stores every setting as a preset (up to 10), recalled with the number keys. Clear Presets deletes them all.

Advanced

ResponseLogarithmic Response makes the bars respond more to quiet sounds and less to loud ones, like the ear; full scale is the same either way. The analogue meters are always linear, like real ones.
BinningEach bar combines several of the 512 spectrum values the host supplies. They are averaged, or with Bin Using Peak Values the largest is used, giving a twitchier display.
DecayExponential Decay imitates an RC metering circuit; otherwise bars fall at a constant rate. Peak Hold Time, Peak Decay Time and Bar Decay Time set the ballistics.
VU metersVU Decay Time sets the analogue needle's response (a real VU meter takes 300 ms); the Gain knob trims meters that read too high or low. The PEAK light shows the fastest peaks above 95% of full scale, held for half a second.
Prevent SleepKeeps the display awake while the visualizer is showing and music is playing.
Spectrum GainNew in 3.1. Scales the spectrum data from Music / iTunes before display. Use it if the bars sit higher or lower in one app than in the other; each app keeps its own setting.
DiagnosticsNew in 3.1. Shows what the host is delivering: frame rate, pulse rate, audio format, spectrum and waveform levels.

About the display

The visualizer does not perform its own FFT; it works only with the 512-point, 8-bit spectrum and waveform data Music or iTunes supplies, so treat it as an attractive display rather than a measuring instrument. Bands are mapped onto that data exactly as LED Spectrum Analyser 2.x and 3.x did.
